Drag each label to the box with the matching number. Then press Check.

    All 13 labels are right. The right side (the person’s right) pumps deoxygenated blood, and the left side pumps oxygenated blood. The atria are at the top and the ventricles are at the bottom, and the ventricles have thicker walls. The valves are one-way and prevent backflow. The septum is the muscular wall that separates the right and left sides.

    Part 5: Blood typing lab

    Read: Your red blood cells carry markers on their surface called antigens. The two main markers are A and B. You may have A, B, both (AB), or neither (O). A third marker, Rh, makes your type positive (+) if you have it and negative (−) if you don’t.

    To find a blood type, scientists add drops of anti-serum to a blood sample. Anti-A serum makes blood clump if the cells have the A marker. Anti-B clumps cells with the B marker. Anti-Rh clumps cells with the Rh marker. Smooth blood means that marker is not there.

    Test each patient’s blood. Add all three serums, then choose the blood type.

    Priya is type B−. She needs blood after a car accident. Which donor bags are safe for her? Choose every safe bag.

    Her immune system attacks any marker that her own red blood cells don’t have.

    Right. Priya’s cells have the B marker and no Rh marker. O− blood has no markers at all, and B− has only B, so neither one brings a marker her body would attack. That is why O− is called the universal donor.
